🎯 Why orchestrator? (90.2% better)

Anthropic measured: multi-agent (Opus 4 lead + Sonnet 4 workers) outperforms single-agent in 90.2% of search tasks. Why does this happen and how to apply it.

📊 The number 90.2% — where does it come from

In 2025, Anthropic published internal evaluation results: a multi-agent system with Opus 4 as lead and Sonnet 4 as workers outperformed a single agent in90.2% of research tasks. It's not marketing — it's a reproducible metric with public eval.

🔬How the assessment was carried out

  • •Reference task:multi-step research with synthesis of multiple sources.
  • •Baseline:single-agent Opus 4 with access to the same tools.
  • •Multi-agent:lead Opus 4 decomp + 3-5 workers Sonnet 4 in parallel.
  • •Metric:precision, completeness and consistency in the final synthesis.
  • •Result:90.2% of multi-agent rounds produced superior output.

📊Marco and Sally: before × after

Marco (e-commerce) asked for competitive analysis.Before:single-agent queried 5 sources sequentially in 12 min, lost context at the end.After:lead decomposed into 3 subproblems, workers researched in parallel, synthesis in 4 min with 3x greater coverage.

"The difference wasn't the model — it was the architecture." — pattern observed in multiple real cases.

2

🌳 Plan × execution — lead × workers

The fundamental division of the orchestrator is betweenthink and do. The lead agent doesn't execute — he decomposes, plans and synthesizes. Workers don't plan — they execute atomic tasks with total focus. This separation of responsibilities is what eliminates the cognitive bottleneck.

The plan-execute-synthesize cycle

  • 1. Decomposition— Lead breaks the task into independent and parallelizable subtasks.
  • 2. Dispatch— Lead sends each subtask to the most appropriate worker (by tool or domain).
  • 3. Parallel execution— Workers run without waiting for each other. Reduces total latency.
  • 4. Collection— Lead receives results from all workers with timestamp and context.
  • 5. Synthesis— Lead consolidates, resolves contradictions and produces coherent final output.

💡Why can't it be the same agent

A single agent trying to plan and execute at the same timeloses planning context during execution. He begins to "forget" the original plan as he delves into the details. Separating the roles keeps the strategic vision intact in the lead.

3

⚖️ Cost × gain — not free

Multi-agent is not a free lunch. The actual coordination overhead:5-15x more tokensthan single-agent in the same task. The question is not "to use it or not" — it is to know when the gain in quality justifies the additional cost.

✓ Multi-agent value when

  • ✓Task has parallelizable subproblems
  • ✓Errors have a high cost (financial, reputational)
  • ✓Research requires broad coverage of sources
  • ✓SLA allows for higher latency (>30s acceptable)
  • ✓Distinct specialized domains

✗ Not valid when

  • ✗Task is simple and atomic
  • ✗Latency is critical (<3s required)
  • ✗Token budget is very restricted
  • ✗Context cannot be split
  • ✗A single specialist can do the trick
4

🔀 Parallelism as a superpower

The most immediate gain from multi-agent is not quality — it isspeed by parallelism. Tasks that would take 20 minutes sequentially take 5 minutes when 4 workers operate simultaneously. For Sally (consultant), this transforms the analysis time from “one day” to “one hour”.

⚡Types of parallelism

  • Simple fan-out— same task sent to multiple workers with variations in perspective.
  • Division by domain— Workers specialized by area: financial, operational, commercial.
  • Division by source— each worker searches a different source or database.
  • Pipeline with stages— worker A processes, worker B validates, worker C formats.

📊Market Data 2025-2026

According to Sequoia Capital (Ascent 2025): companies that adopted multi-agent architecture reduced research time by68%on average. Gartner (Jan/2026) projects that 40% of analytical workflows will use multi-agent by the end of 2026.

5

🧠 Isolated context = less hallucination

One of the least discussed benefits of multi-agent is thehallucination reduction by context isolation. Each worker operates with a small, focused context. Without the "noise" of other subtasks in context, the chance of confusion and mixing of information drops dramatically.

Why smaller context is better

  • Focus— worker only sees the task he needs to solve, without distraction.
  • Less interference— irrelevant information does not “contaminate” reasoning.
  • Fresh context— each worker starts without the weight of previous iterations.
  • Verifiability— result of each worker is individually auditable.

💡Human analogy

A CFO who also tries to do marketing and operations at the same time makes worse decisions across the board. Specialization improves quality — it goes for humans, it goes for agents.

6

🔄 Eval-driven: measure before you scale

Don't add workers out of intuition.Measure first. The correct pattern is: single-agent works → measures results → identifies where it fails → adds specific worker to cover that weak point. Without eval, you add complexity for no real gain.

📐The eval-driven process

  • Step 1:Defines the task and success criteria (e.g. “analysis covers 5+ distinct sources”).
  • Step 2:Run single-agent 10 times. Measure success rate.
  • Step 3:Identifies the failure pattern (e.g. "always loses financial analysis").
  • Step 4:Adds specialized worker to cover weak point.
  • Step 5:Measure again. Compare cost × quality gain.

⚠️Anti-pattern: orchestrator without eval

Sana (founder) set up a system with 8 workers before measuring the single-agent. It spent 12x the budget and the result was worse — the workers contradicted each other without clear synthesis criteria. Starting with eval would have shown that 2 workers were enough.
