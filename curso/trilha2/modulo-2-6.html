🪪 Identity > prompt: the "passwd" of the agentic era

Who is the agent, what is its jurisdiction, what is its scope. Identity comes before any instruction — it's what remains when the prompt disappears.

🆔 Identity × prompt

A prompt is a temporary instruction: "review this contract", "refactor this function", "generate monthly report". When he's done, he leaves.Identity is different: is what the agent is in every session, in every prompt, regardless of what you ask. It is the substrate that persists when the context of the conversation disappears.

🔄The fundamental difference

  • Prompt: single instruction, volatile, changes every session, no guaranteed persistence.
  • Identity: persistent contract, versioned in git, defines who the agent is and what never changes.
  • In practice:prompt says "what to do now". Identity says “who you are always”.

The passwd analogy

On Unix, the file/etc/passwddefines who users are — name, home, shell — regardless of what they type. CLAUDE.md is the passwd of the agentic era: it defines the agent's identity before any interaction. You can give a thousand different prompts; the identity persists in all of them.

2

📛 Name, role and SLA

An agent's "identity card" goes beyond their name. She definesrole, jurisdiction and SLA— the three pillars that transform a generic LLM into a collaborator with a defined role. An agent without this license is like an employee without a contract: you don't know what he does, you don't know where he operates, and there's no way to charge performance.

Examples of identity cards

  • CFO Bot (Marco): "I analyze financial data from e-commerce. I only access /finance/. I respond within 2 minutes. I do not make payment decisions without approval."
  • Legal Researcher (Sally): "I research jurisprudence and legislation. Access /cases/cliente-ativo/ and legal bases. I respond within 5 minutes. I do not offer legal advice — analysis only."
  • Clinical Assistant (Dr. Sana): "Differential diagnostic support. Access only /data/clinical/patient-active/. I never externalize data. I always recommend medical confirmation."

💡Why SLA matters

SLA is not just a time expectation — it is a quality contract. Defines when the agent should escalate to human (complexity above SLA), what is acceptable as a response (analysis vs decision) and how to measure performance (average time, % escalations).

3

🔑 Identity vs Authentication

In systems security, confusing identity with authentication is a classic source of vulnerabilities.Identityis "who the agent is" — defined by CLAUDE.md, stable, persistent.Authenticationit's "how he proves he is who he says he is" — tokens, API keys, certificates. ANDauthorizationit's "what he can do" — permissions, jurisdiction. The three are distinct and need to be managed separately.

The three pillars — identity × authn × authz

  • Identity (who I am): CLAUDE.md, name, function, jurisdiction. Set by configuration, not by credential.
  • Authentication (how I prove it): API keys, OAuth tokens, certificates. Secrets in vault, never on CLAUDE.md.
  • Authorization (what can I do): settings.json, filesystem permissions, allowlist/denylist.

⚠️Classic vulnerability

Put authentication tokens in CLAUDE.md to "make it easier". CLAUDE.md goes to git. Git goes to the repository. The repository can be public. Result: leaked credentials, compromised system. Identity and authentication live in different places for good reasons.

4

📇 Agent Cards (A2A)

Google's A2A (Agent-to-Agent) protocol introduced the concept ofAgent Cards— a standardized format for agents to advertise their capabilities, available authentication, and communication endpoints. It's the equivalent of a business card in JSON: when an agent meets another, they present their card and both immediately know how to communicate.

What does an Agent Card contain

  • •name + description: who is the agent, what does it do in natural language.
  • •capabilities: list of skills and tasks that the agent can perform.
  • •authentication: supported methods (OAuth, API key, mTLS).
  • •endpoints: where to send tasks, how to receive callbacks.
  • •constraints: what the agent does not do (jurisdiction scoped).

Adoption in 2026

A2A with Agent Cards is the most adopted agent discovery protocol in 2026 — 150+ partners, 22k+ stars on GitHub. Those who build Agent Cards today participate in the automatic discovery ecosystem: your agent can be found and hired by other agents without manual configuration.

5

🔄 Identity lives in version control

CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md need to be in git — this is not a suggestion, it is an obligation.Identity cannot change without a trace. If someone changes an agent's non-negotiable rules without going through a PR review, you don't know when it happened, who did it, or why. In production, this is unacceptable. In regulated sectors, it is a control violation.

Git-backed workflow for identity

  • 1. Proposed change: create branch feature/claude-md-update with diff.
  • 2. PR review: At least one reviewer reviews the rule change.
  • 3. Discussion: reason documented in PR description.
  • 4. Merge: approved, merge into main, takes effect in the next session.
  • 5. Traceability: git blame shows who changed each line, when, why.

💡Identity Rollback

With CLAUDE.md in git, reverting to a previous identity version is agit revert. If a rule change caused unexpected behavior, you restore the previous state in seconds — with complete record of what the rollback happened and why.

6

🎓 When to create a new identity

Creating a new agent — a new identity — has real operational costs: maintaining CLAUDE.md, permissions, logs, SLA, training those who use it.Don't create agents for the sake of fad. Create when there is real load and a genuinely distinct function that justifies the overhead. The YAGNI (You Ain't Gonna Need It) principle applies here with full force.

✓ Create new agent when...

  • ✓Function is distinct and specialized
  • ✓Volume justifies full dedication
  • ✓Different jurisdiction and data
  • ✓Necessary risk isolation
  • ✓SLA different from other agents

✗ Do not create an agent when...

  • ✗Task is occasional or seasonal
  • ✗A skill resolves without overhead
  • ✗Function overlaps with existing agent
  • ✗Just to look "more advanced"
  • ✗No defined human owner

Consolidation vs Proliferation

Marco started with 8 agents. After 3 months, it consolidated to 4 — two were redundant, two others rarely used. Result: less maintenance overhead, more focus, better quality. Fewer well-defined agents outperform many vague agents. Periodically ask yourself: "Does this agent still justify its existence?"
